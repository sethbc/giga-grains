# M2 carry-over: what M1 leaves open

**The policy was retired on 2026-10-30** (the director): deferred items are handled as superpowers'
subagent-driven development handles them. A reviewer's minor is fixed or deferred at the controller's
judgment; deferred minors and items blocked outside our control are listed in the sub-project's final
report and recorded here as a plain log. There is no count rule and no "touch it, close it" rule. A row
stays until the thing it names is done, and nothing is filed upstream. The M1 policy's text is kept in
`docs/m1-carry-over.md` as history.

*Made on 2026-10-29, when the carry-over was split for M2 (the director, during the
release-pipeline work): the 13 rows `docs/m1-carry-over.md` still held open moved here, and that
file became M1's frozen record. Nothing else moved; the M1 file keeps every closed row and the
history of each.*

Each row keeps the number it had in the M1 file, the line it stood on there before that file was
frozen, which the reviews, INTENT and the specs cite ("row 221"), with a pointer back to it. Its
text, source and "When" are as they stood at the split. The IDs in the Source column ([297],
A-m6, T4 (127), S8, PU-T11a and the rest) are explained at the top of the M1 file and of each of
its sections. A row added from now on takes the next free number from 300 up, so no number names
two rows. A closed row stays in place, marked **Closed** in the "When" column with the commits
that closed it, as in the M1 file.

**The count.** 13 open rows at the split; 11 after M2 sub-project 1 (rows 118 and 119). M2
sub-project 3 closed row 51 and added rows 300 to 310; M2 sub-project 5 added rows 233 and 234, numbered
before the rule above (no M1 row has either number). The count rules are retired (the
policy above, INTENT §23 #67), so nothing here is counted against anything.
